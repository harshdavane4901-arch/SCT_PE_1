# Example 1: Climate Change

## 1. Original Prompt

Explain climate change.

## 2. Original Output

Climate change refers to long-term changes in Earth's temperature and weather patterns. It is mainly caused today by human activities such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial pollution. These activities release greenhouse gases, such as carbon dioxide, that trap heat in the atmosphere.

## 3. Improved Prompt

Explain climate change in 3 bullet points for a 12-year-old student. Use simple English, explain one main cause and one major effect, and include one practical action students can take to help the environment.

## 4. Improved Output

- What is climate change? Climate change means long-term changes in Earth's temperature and weather patterns. Earth is getting warmer because of human activities.

- Cause and effect: Burning coal, petrol, and diesel releases gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. This causes global warming, which can lead to melting ice, rising sea levels, and extreme weather.


How can students help? Plant trees, save electricity, and walk or cycle for short trips when safe. Small actions can help protect our planet!

## 5. Comparison and Analysis

- **Clarity:** The improved prompt clearly explains the task.
- **Specificity:** It requests exactly three bullet points and identifies the target audience.
- **Tone:** It asks for simple English suitable for younger students.
- **Result:** The original prompt requests a general explanation, while the improved prompt specifies the audience, format, and practical advice.

# Example 2: Study Tips

## 1. Original Prompt

Give me study tips.

## 2. Original Output

Effective study tips include creating a study schedule, taking regular breaks, reviewing notes, practising questions, and getting enough sleep. Students can improve their learning by studying in a quiet place and avoiding distractions.

## 3. Improved Prompt

Give a beginner student 5 practical study tips for preparing for an upcoming exam. Use simple English, number the tips from 1 to 5, include one short example for each tip, and keep the tone friendly and encouraging.

## 4. Improved Output

1. **Create a Study Schedule:** Plan what you will study each day. Example: Study mathematics on Monday and science on Tuesday.

2. **Take Short Breaks:** Take breaks between study sessions to refresh your mind. Example: Study for 25 minutes and then take a 5-minute break.

3. **Practise Questions:** Solve practice questions to understand topics better. Example: Complete five questions after studying a chapter.

4. **Revise Regularly:** Review your notes to remember important information. Example: Spend 10 minutes revising yesterday's lesson.

5. **Avoid Distractions:** Study in a quiet place and keep your phone away. Example: Turn off notifications while studying.

## 5. Comparison and Analysis

- **Clarity:** The improved prompt clearly asks for exam preparation advice.
- **Specificity:** It requests five numbered tips with one example for each.
- **Tone:** It asks for a friendly and encouraging response.
- **Result:** The original prompt produces general advice, while the improved prompt requests organized tips and practical examples.

# Example 3: Professional Email

## 1. Original Prompt

Write an email.

## 2. Original Output

Subject: Meeting Request

Dear Sir/Madam,

I hope you are doing well. I am writing to request a meeting with you. Please let me know a suitable date and time for the meeting.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,  
[Your Name]

## 3. Improved Prompt

Write a professional email to a company HR representative requesting an update on my internship application. Use a polite and respectful tone, include a clear subject line, keep the message between 100 and 150 words, and end with a professional closing. Do not invent personal details.

## 4. Improved Output

Subject: Request for Update on Internship Application

Dear HR Team,

I hope you are doing well.

I am writing to kindly inquire about the status of my internship application. I am interested in the opportunity to learn and develop my skills through your organization, and I would appreciate any updates you could share regarding the application process.

I understand that reviewing applications takes time, and I appreciate your consideration. Please let me know if any additional information or documents are required from my side.

Thank you for your time and support. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards,  
[Your Name]

## 5. Comparison and Analysis

- **Clarity:** The improved prompt explains the purpose of the email.
- **Specificity:** It identifies the recipient, subject, word limit, and closing.
- **Tone:** It requests professional and respectful language.
- **Result:** The original prompt leaves the email's purpose unspecified, while the improved prompt describes a particular professional situation.
